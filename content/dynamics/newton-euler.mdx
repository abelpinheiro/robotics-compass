## Formal definition
To model a robot's dynamics, we need three physical parameters for each of its links:

{/* TODO(author): "weight" below: mass and weight are different quantities. */}

1. **Mass ($m_i$)**: The weight of the link.
2. **Center of Mass (${}^{i}P_{C_i}$)**: The 3D vector pointing to the balance point of the link.
3. **Inertia Tensor (${}^{C_i}I_i$)**: A $3 \times 3$ matrix describing how the link's mass is distributed around its center of mass, dictating how hard it is to rotate.

### Single rigid body

{/* TODO(author): explain Newton's and Euler's equations for one link. Becomes a short review once "Rigid bodies and inertia" is published. */}

$$
F = m\,\dot v_{C}, \qquad N = {}^{C}I\,\dot\omega + \omega \times {}^{C}I\,\omega
$$

where $\dot v_{C}$ is the centre-of-mass acceleration and ${}^{C}I$ is the inertia tensor about the COM.

### The Recursive Newton-Euler Algorithm
Just as we used Velocity Propagation to find the Jacobian recursively, we use the **Newton-Euler formulation** to recursively compute joint torques. It is split into two passes:

### Outward recursion (base → tip)

We start at the base and move to the end-effector. We propagate the angular and linear velocities ($\omega_i, v_i$), but now we also compute their derivatives: angular acceleration ($\dot{\omega}_i$) and linear acceleration ($\dot{v}_i$). Finally, we calculate the linear acceleration of each link's center of mass ($\dot{v}_{C_i}$).

{/* TODO(author): explain how velocities and accelerations propagate outward. */}

For revolute joint $i{+}1$ with axis $\hat Z_{i+1}$:

$$
{}^{i+1}\omega_{i+1} = {}^{i+1}_{i}R\,{}^{i}\omega_i + \dot\theta_{i+1}\,{}^{i+1}\hat Z_{i+1}
$$

$$
{}^{i+1}\dot\omega_{i+1} = {}^{i+1}_{i}R\,{}^{i}\dot\omega_i + {}^{i+1}_{i}R\,{}^{i}\omega_i \times \dot\theta_{i+1}\,{}^{i+1}\hat Z_{i+1} + \ddot\theta_{i+1}\,{}^{i+1}\hat Z_{i+1}
$$

$$
{}^{i+1}\dot v_{i+1} = {}^{i+1}_{i}R\left( {}^{i}\dot\omega_i \times {}^{i}P_{i+1} + {}^{i}\omega_i \times \left( {}^{i}\omega_i \times {}^{i}P_{i+1} \right) + {}^{i}\dot v_i \right)
$$

{/* TODO(author): prismatic joint. */}

$$
{}^{i+1}\omega_{i+1} = {}^{i+1}_{i}R\,{}^{i}\omega_i, \qquad {}^{i+1}\dot\omega_{i+1} = {}^{i+1}_{i}R\,{}^{i}\dot\omega_i
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
{}^{i+1}\dot v_{i+1} ={}& {}^{i+1}_{i}R\left( {}^{i}\dot\omega_i \times {}^{i}P_{i+1} + {}^{i}\omega_i \times \left( {}^{i}\omega_i \times {}^{i}P_{i+1} \right) + {}^{i}\dot v_i \right) \\
&+ 2\,{}^{i+1}\omega_{i+1} \times \dot d_{i+1}\,{}^{i+1}\hat Z_{i+1} + \ddot d_{i+1}\,{}^{i+1}\hat Z_{i+1}
\end{aligned}
$$

{/* TODO(author): centre of mass of link i (either joint type). */}

$$
{}^{i}\dot v_{C_i} = {}^{i}\dot\omega_i \times {}^{i}P_{C_i} + {}^{i}\omega_i \times \left( {}^{i}\omega_i \times {}^{i}P_{C_i} \right) + {}^{i}\dot v_i
$$

### Inward recursion (tip → base)

We start at the end-effector and move backward to the base. First, we apply Newton's and Euler's equations to find the inertial force ($F_i$) and moment ($N_i$) acting on each link's center of mass:

$$
{}^{i}F_i = m_i\,{}^{i}\dot v_{C_i}, \qquad {}^{i}N_i = {}^{C_i}I_i\,{}^{i}\dot\omega_i + {}^{i}\omega_i \times {}^{C_i}I_i\,{}^{i}\omega_i
$$

Then, we balance the forces acting on the joints. The force and torque exerted by link $i$ on link $i+1$ are computed backward down the chain. The Z-component of the resulting joint torque vector gives us the physical motor torque ($\tau_i$) required at that joint.

#### Static case

{/* TODO(author): the robot at rest, without gravity, with a force at the tip: F_i = N_i = 0. The result must match the Jacobian duality. F is the force the robot applies on the environment. */}

$$
{}^{i}f_i = {}^{i}_{i+1}R\,{}^{i+1}f_{i+1}, \qquad {}^{i}n_i = {}^{i}_{i+1}R\,{}^{i+1}n_{i+1} + {}^{i}P_{i+1} \times {}^{i}_{i+1}R\,{}^{i+1}f_{i+1}
$$

$$
\tau_i = {}^{i}n_i^{T}\,{}^{i}\hat Z_i
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\tau = J^{T}F
$$

#### Moving links

{/* TODO(author): explain how forces and moments propagate inward to give joint torques. */}

$$
{}^{i}f_i = {}^{i}_{i+1}R\,{}^{i+1}f_{i+1} + {}^{i}F_i
$$

$$
{}^{i}n_i = {}^{i}N_i + {}^{i}_{i+1}R\,{}^{i+1}n_{i+1} + {}^{i}P_{C_i} \times {}^{i}F_i + {}^{i}P_{i+1} \times {}^{i}_{i+1}R\,{}^{i+1}f_{i+1}
$$

$$
\tau_i = {}^{i}n_i^{T}\,{}^{i}\hat Z_i \ \text{(revolute)}, \qquad \tau_i = {}^{i}f_i^{T}\,{}^{i}\hat Z_i \ \text{(prismatic)}
$$

### Boundary conditions

{/* TODO(author): explain the base/tip conditions and the gravity trick. */}

$$
{}^{0}\omega_0 = {}^{0}\dot\omega_0 = 0, \qquad {}^{0}\dot v_0 = -\,\mathbf{g}, \qquad {}^{n+1}f_{n+1} = {}^{n+1}n_{n+1} = 0
$$

Setting the base acceleration to $-\mathbf{g}$ folds gravity into the recursion (the "gravity trick"). Collecting the joint torques over all configurations yields the standard form developed in Equations of motion:

{/* TODO: link "Equations of motion" again when that lesson is published (it has no page in production yet). */}

$$
M(q)\,\ddot q + C(q,\dot q)\,\dot q + g(q) = \tau
$$

$$
I = (0.08,\ 0.05)\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2}, \quad g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}

$$
\theta = (30^\circ,\ 60^\circ), \qquad \dot\theta = (1,\ 2)\ \mathrm{rad/s}, \qquad \ddot\theta = (1,\ 1)\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}, \qquad {}^{0}\dot v_0 = [\,0,\ 9.81,\ 0\,]^{T}
$$

| | Link 1 | Link 2 |
|---|---|---|
| $({}^i\omega_i)_z$ | 1 | 3 |
| $({}^i\dot\omega_i)_z$ | 1 | 2 |
| $({}^i\dot v_i)_{xy}$ | (4.905, 8.496) | (10.176, 1.366) |
| $({}^i\dot v_{C_i})_{xy}$ | (4.405, 8.996) | (6.576, 2.166) |

\tau = \begin{bmatrix} 18.349 \\ 1.140 \end{bmatrix} \mathrm{N\,m}

For a planar 2R arm (link lengths $l_1, l_2$; COM distances $l_{c1}, l_{c2}$; masses $m_1, m_2$; COM inertias $I_1, I_2$; gravity $g$), the recursion collapses to a closed form:

$$
M(q) = \begin{bmatrix}
m_1 l_{c1}^2 + I_1 + m_2\!\left(l_1^2 + l_{c2}^2 + 2 l_1 l_{c2}\cos\theta_2\right) + I_2 & m_2\!\left(l_{c2}^2 + l_1 l_{c2}\cos\theta_2\right) + I_2 \\
m_2\!\left(l_{c2}^2 + l_1 l_{c2}\cos\theta_2\right) + I_2 & m_2 l_{c2}^2 + I_2
\end{bmatrix}
$$

With $h = m_2 l_1 l_{c2}\sin\theta_2$:

$$
C(q,\dot q)\,\dot q = \begin{bmatrix} -h\,\dot\theta_2^2 - 2 h\,\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2 \\ h\,\dot\theta_1^2 \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
g(q) = \begin{bmatrix} (m_1 l_{c1} + m_2 l_1)\,g\cos\theta_1 + m_2 l_{c2}\,g\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) \\ m_2 l_{c2}\,g\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) \end{bmatrix}
$$
