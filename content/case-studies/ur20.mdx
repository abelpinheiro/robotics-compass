## Overview & specifications

The UR20 is Universal Robots' heavy-duty collaborative robot, fundamentally redesigned from their classic e-Series line to handle high-torque tasks. Operating as a 6-DOF manipulator, it features a 20 kg payload capacity and a massive 1750 mm reach, while maintaining a relatively low physical mass of 64 kg. 

This case study will walk through its complete kinematic and dynamic profile, from constructing its Denavit-Hartenberg matrices to computing the Yoshikawa manipulability index to ensure safe trajectory planning near its singularity boundaries.

| Specification | Value |
| :-- | :-- |
| Degrees of freedom | 6 (all revolute) |
| Reach | 1750 mm |
| Payload | 20 kg |

| Joint | a [m] | d [m] | α [rad] | θ |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 1 | 0 | 0.2363 | π/2 | θ₁ |
| 2 | −0.8620 | 0 | 0 | θ₂ |
| 3 | −0.7287 | 0 | 0 | θ₃ |
| 4 | 0 | 0.2010 | π/2 | θ₄ |
| 5 | 0 | 0.1593 | −π/2 | θ₅ |
| 6 | 0 | 0.1543 | 0 | θ₆ |

Looking closely at the UR20's table reveals its physical design:
*   **The Upper Arm and Forearm ($a_2$ and $a_3$):** The only non-zero values in the $a$ column are the physical lengths of the main arm links. They are negative ($-0.8620$ m and $-0.7287$ m) because the $X$-axes of frames 2 and 3 are assigned to point in the opposite direction of the links when the robot is in its zero configuration.
*   **The Shoulder and Wrist Offsets ($d$ column):** The $d$ values represent lateral displacements along the joint axes. $d_1$ ($0.2363$ m) is the height of the shoulder. The wrist offsets ($d_4, d_5, d_6$) create the characteristic "kinked" geometry of a UR wrist, ensuring the final three joint axes intersect in a way that allows for a closed-form analytical Inverse Kinematics solution.

## Forward kinematics

{/* TODO(author): explain how FK chains the per-joint transforms and what ⁰₆T represents. */}

Per-joint transform (classic DH), using the parameters from the table above:

$$
{}^{i-1}_{i}T(\theta_i) = \begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta_i & -\sin\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & \sin\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\cos\theta_i \\
\sin\theta_i & \cos\theta_i\cos\alpha_i & -\cos\theta_i\sin\alpha_i & a_i\sin\theta_i \\
0 & \sin\alpha_i & \cos\alpha_i & d_i \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The base-to-tool transform is their product:

$$
{}^{0}_{6}T = {}^{0}_{1}T\,{}^{1}_{2}T\,{}^{2}_{3}T\,{}^{3}_{4}T\,{}^{4}_{5}T\,{}^{5}_{6}T = \begin{bmatrix} {}^{0}_{6}R & {}^{0}P_{6} \\ \mathbf{0} & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

## Inverse kinematics

{/* TODO(author): explain the UR20 IK — the analytic closed-form solution and/or the numerical approach, and the multiple solution branches. */}

Numerical IK by damped least squares (position), iterated toward a Cartesian target $x_{\text{target}}$:

$$
q_{k+1} = q_k + J_v^{\top}\left(J_v J_v^{\top} + \lambda^2 I\right)^{-1}\left(x_{\text{target}} - x_{\text{current}}\right)
$$

where $J_v$ is the linear (position) block of the geometric Jacobian and $\lambda$ damps the update near singularities.

## Velocity kinematics & the Jacobian

{/* TODO(author): construct the UR20 Jacobian and the joint-velocity ↔ tool-velocity mapping. */}

The geometric Jacobian $J(q)$ maps joint rates $\dot q$ to the tool twist (linear velocity $v$ and angular velocity $\omega$):

$$
\begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix} = J(q)\,\dot q, \qquad
J(q) = \begin{bmatrix} J_v \\ J_\omega \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times 6}.
$$

In the classic indexing joint $i$ acts about $z_{i-1}$. Every UR20 joint is revolute, so for joint $i$ with axis $z_{i-1}$ and frame origin $o_{i-1}$ (both in the base frame, read from the forward-kinematics chain), and tool position $o_6$, the $i$-th column is

$$
J_{:,i} = \begin{bmatrix} z_{i-1} \times (o_6 - o_{i-1}) \\ z_{i-1} \end{bmatrix}.
$$

The image of the linear block $J_v$ over unit-norm joint rates is the **velocity manipulability ellipsoid**; its semi-axes are the singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \sigma_3$ of $J_v$, and the Yoshikawa index $w = \lvert\det J\rvert$ measures the volume of dexterity.

## Singularities & workspace

{/* TODO(author): analyze the UR20's singular configurations and reachable workspace. */}

A configuration $q$ is **singular** when the Jacobian loses rank — equivalently, when the manipulability drops to zero:

$$
\operatorname{rank} J(q) < 6 \iff \det J(q) = 0 \iff w(q) = 0.
$$

There the smallest singular value of $J$ goes to zero and the tool loses the ability to move (or reorient) in at least one direction. The UR20 has three singularity families:

- **Wrist** — $\theta_5 = 0$, so joint axes 4 and 6 become collinear (a lost *orientation* freedom).
- **Elbow** — the arm is fully extended, $\theta_3 = 0$.
- **Shoulder** — the wrist centre lies on the axis of joint 1.

The reachable workspace is bounded by the 1750 mm reach. The heatmap below plots $w$ over a 2-joint slice of configuration space; the singular manifolds are the dark curves where $w \to 0$.
